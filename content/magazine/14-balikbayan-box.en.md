# Balikbayan box shipping guide: rules, limits, and Christmas timing

_From Pinoy Pages 2026–2027_

A **balikbayan box** is how many OFWs and immigrants send clothes, groceries and household goods home. Philippine rules decide what enters **duty- and tax-free**, and what is **prohibited or restricted**. This guide is based on **Customs Administrative Order (CAO) No. 01-2018** (amended rules on consolidated balikbayan boxes) as published through the Supreme Court E-Library, and on **Republic Act No. 10863** (Customs Modernization and Tariff Act / CMTA) Sections **118** and **119**. It does **not** invent a single government “Christmas cut-off” date.

## Who can use the duty and tax privilege

Under CAO 01-2018, **Qualified Filipinos While Abroad** (non-resident Filipinos who kept Philippine citizenship, OFWs, and resident Filipinos temporarily abroad such as on student, investor or tourist visas) may send balikbayan boxes to **family or relatives** (up to the fourth civil degree of consanguinity or affinity).

**Sole proprietorships and companies are not** qualified for this privilege.

## Core limits (CAO 01-2018)

- **Up to three (3) availments per calendar year** (January 1–December 31). Boxes you bring as accompanied or unaccompanied baggage as a passenger also count toward the three.
- **Total FCA value** of all balikbayan boxes per sender in a calendar year must **not exceed Php 150,000**.
- Contents must be **personal and household effects only** — **not** in commercial quantities, and **not** for barter, sale or hire.
- A shipment above the **de minimis** value of **Php 10,000** is automatically **one availment**. De minimis importations are not counted toward the three, with the conditions in the CAO.
- One consolidated shipment under one master bill of lading / master air waybill counts as **one availment**, even if there are several boxes or several ultimate consignees in that shipment (subject to the CAO’s rules on one ultimate consignee per sender in a consolidated shipment as defined there).
- Maximum volume per box/container under the CAO definition: **200,000 gross cubic centimeters**.
- A processing charge of **Php 125** per house bill of lading / house air waybill (as stated in CAO 01-2018; the Commissioner may revise amounts) applies for the privilege.

You must accomplish the BOC **Information Sheet** (packing list) with sender and consignee details, itemized contents and values, number of availments that year, and supporting ID as listed in the CAO, and give it to your consolidator so it reaches the Philippines **before** arrival within the timelines the CAO sets for sea and air.

## What is excluded from the simplified / duty-free treatment

CAO 01-2018 excludes, among other cases: shipments whose contents exceed Php 150,000 FCA; senders who already used up the Php 150,000 or the three availments; goods in **commercial quantity** (except if they qualify as de minimis); regulated goods in commercial quantity; donations; mixed containers (for example donations mixed with personal effects); and failure to meet sender documentation obligations.

## What you cannot ship (CMTA)

### Prohibited (CMTA Sec. 118) — examples

Importation (and exportation) of goods such as:

- Printed matter advocating treason, rebellion, sedition, or threats of violence against persons in the Philippines
- Goods/instruments/drugs intended for unlawful abortion, and related advertising matter
- Obscene or immoral writings, films, images or objects
- Precious-metal goods with false fineness marks
- Adulterated or misbranded food or drugs
- Infringing (counterfeit / pirated) goods under intellectual property law
- Other goods explicitly prohibited by law or competent authority

### Restricted (CMTA Sec. 119) — need legal authorization

Except when authorized by law or regulation, restricted goods include:

- Dynamite, gunpowder, ammunition, explosives, firearms and weapons of war (or parts)
- Gambling outfits and chance-based distribution machines (and parts)
- Lottery and sweepstakes tickets (with limited exceptions for ads/draw lists)
- Narcotics and habit-forming drugs (except government / Dangerous Drugs Board–authorized medicinal imports)
- Opium pipes or parts
- Other goods whose import/export is restricted

Restriction also covers **transit** of those goods.

**Practical packing tip from the rules:** stick to personal and household effects in non-commercial quantities; declare everything honestly; ask your forwarder which **regulated** items (food, cosmetics, electronics, etc.) need permits. When in doubt, leave it out.

## Christmas timing — what we can and cannot say

**We did not find a single Bureau of Customs “ship by this date for Christmas” deadline** on the official pages we could retrieve. Cut-off dates for Christmas delivery are almost always set by **freight forwarders**, based on ocean or air transit times, port congestion and their own sailing schedules.

What you can do with facts you control:

1. Ask your forwarder for **their** Christmas cut-off for your origin city and destination province
2. Remember sea consolidated boxes often take **several weeks**; air costs more
3. Count your **three availments** and **Php 150,000** cap before a big Christmas send
4. Submit a complete Information Sheet early so clearance is not delayed

Pinoy Pages will not invent a national cut-off date. If BOC or DTI publishes a seasonal advisory, we will update this page with a link.
