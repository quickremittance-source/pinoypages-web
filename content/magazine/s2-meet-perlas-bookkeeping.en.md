# A shoebox of receipts, and the bookkeeper who asked for it

Grace Villanueva remembers the shoebox precisely. It was a winter-boot box, size eight, and it contained eleven months of receipts for her cousin's catering business, most of them thermal paper already fading to grey.

"She handed it to me at a birthday party and said, 'You're good with numbers, di ba?'" Villanueva recalls. "That was my first client. I didn't charge her, which I now consider a professional error."

Nine years later, [Perlas Bookkeeping & Payroll](/listing/biz-01) occupies a shared office on Denison Street in Markham and keeps the books for an eclectic roster: a Filipino eatery, two residential cleaning companies, a dental clinic and a handful of tradespeople. She can tell you, without consulting a ledger, which of them pays promptly.

Her pricing is fixed and monthly, a decision she made after watching small-business owners avoid calling their accountants for fear of being billed by the quarter-hour. "If you're afraid to ask me a question, I'm useless to you," she says. Clients text her on Thursday evenings, when the office stays open until eight, and she answers.

The work itself is unglamorous and exacting. Bank reconciliations. Payroll remittances. Quarterly HST returns, filed before the deadline rather than on it. For newer clients who are unsure whether they need to register for HST at all, she begins with the [HST checker](/tools/hst-checker) and a frank conversation about where their revenue is heading.

Her more evangelical moments concern record-keeping. She has a standing offer for any new client who arrives with a shoebox: she will sort it, and then she will teach them never to need one again. Most people, she says, need about three months to acquire the habit of photographing a receipt the moment it is printed.

She vouches for, and is vouched for by, Maria Santos of [Bituin Tax & Bookkeeping](/listing/tax-01), who prepares the personal returns of several of her clients, and Mark Garcia of [Kidlat IT Support](/listing/ind-04), who once rescued her server on the morning of a payroll run.

"Mutual vouching sounds like a formality until you think about what it means," she says. "I'm putting my name next to theirs. I don't do that casually."

She is hosting a [networking evening for small-business owners](/calendar/ev-networking-nov) in Markham this November. The cousin, she reports, now keeps immaculate records, and caters the event.
