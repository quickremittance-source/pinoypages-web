# Newcomer's first Canadian tax return: a checklist

_From Pinoy Pages 2026–2027_

Welcome to Canada! Whether you came as a permanent resident, on a work or study permit, or to join family, your **first tax return** matters. It's how the CRA finds out about you and your family, and it's what keeps your benefits coming. **If you arrived in 2026, your 2026 return is due April 30, 2027** (June 15, 2027 to file if you or your spouse were self-employed, but any balance owing is still due April 30).

## ✅ Before you file

- [ ] **Get your Social Insurance Number (SIN)** from Service Canada. If Service Canada can't issue you one, the CRA may give you a **temporary tax number (TTN)**. Keep your SIN private.
- [ ] **Know your date of entry** into Canada. For most newcomers, you become a resident for tax purposes on the first day you live here, but it depends on your **residential ties** (home, spouse or partner, dependants in Canada). Unsure? The CRA has **Form NR74** to help determine your residency status.
- [ ] **Apply for benefits early. You don't have to wait for your first return:**
  - No children: **RC151** (online web form) for the **Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit** (formerly the GST/HST credit). One application per household.
  - With children under 19: if eligible for the Canada Child Benefit, apply with **RC66** (plus **RC66SCH** when required — for example if you or your spouse became a resident in the last two years, became a citizen in the last 12 months, or are a permanent resident / protected person / temporary resident in the situations the CRA lists). RC66 also covers CGEB eligibility. Include **proof of birth** for each child when the CRA has never paid benefits for them.
  - For many newborns, the provincial or territorial **Automated Benefits Application** at birth registration can start CCB — do **not** also re-apply with RC66 if you used that service.
  - See also [Benefits Filipino families often miss](/magazine/benefits-families-miss) for CGEB and CCB amounts and payment timing.
- [ ] **Have your pre-arrival income ready.** The CRA may ask for **world income for you and your spouse for up to two years before you arrived** (a Statement of Income). Convert amounts to Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada exchange rate on the date you received the income. Report it **even if it was zero**. You don't pay Canadian tax on income earned before you became a resident.
- [ ] **Translations:** documents not in English or French need an acceptable translation.

## ✅ Gather your documents

- [ ] **Tax slips**: T4 (employment), T4A, T5 (investment income), T5007, and so on. These usually arrive by the **end of February**.
- [ ] **Income from outside Canada** for the part of the year you were already a resident. You must report it.
- [ ] **Receipts**: childcare, **moving expenses**, medical expenses, charitable donations
- [ ] **Self-employed?** Keep all business receipts and records of your income
- [ ] **Foreign property:** if at any time in the year you owned foreign property **costing more than $100,000**, check the CRA's foreign income verification rules (Form T1135) to see if you need to report it.
- [ ] **Property you owned before arriving**, and any **foreign tax** you paid. Ask about foreign tax credits and treaties.

## ✅ File

- [ ] **Online** with NETFILE-certified software. Some options are free, and first-time filers with a SIN (or a TTN starting with 01 or 03) can usually file online.
- [ ] **Or on paper**, using the T1 package for the province where you lived on December 31
- [ ] **Or with a tax preparer.** See [How to choose a tax preparer](/guides/choose-tax-preparer).
- [ ] **Or at a free tax clinic** (Community Volunteer Income Tax Program) if you have a **modest income and a simple tax situation**
- [ ] **Your spouse or common-law partner files too**, every year, even with little or no income
- [ ] **Sign up for direct deposit** to get refunds and benefits faster

## ✅ After you file

- [ ] Watch for your **Notice of Assessment (NOA)**. Keep it. It's useful for sponsorship, citizenship, mortgage and loan applications.
- [ ] **Register for a CRA account** (My Account) once you have your NOA
- [ ] **Temporary residents:** for the Canada Child Benefit, you generally need to have lived in Canada for the **previous 18 months** with a **valid permit in the 19th month**. **Send the CRA your renewed permit** before the old one expires, or payments stop.
- [ ] **Keep your records for at least six years**

## ⚠️ Watch out for scams

The CRA will **not** demand payment by e-transfer, gift cards or crypto, threaten deportation or arrest, or ask for personal information by email or voicemail. If in doubt, check My Account or call the CRA directly.

## For OFW-background families

If you worked in the Philippines or abroad before coming, keep your employment and contribution records. Canada and the Philippines have a **social security agreement** that may help you qualify for some Canadian pension benefits later. See canada.ca for details.
