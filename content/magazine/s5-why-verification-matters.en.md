# The call that sounded exactly like the CRA

The voice on the line was calm, official and in a hurry. It informed a retired caregiver in Scarborough that she owed back taxes, that a warrant had been prepared, and that the matter could be settled that afternoon with gift cards from the pharmacy across the street.

She very nearly went. What stopped her, she later told the volunteer who runs her seniors' club, was that the caller would not let her hang up to phone her nephew.

The story is a composite, but every element of it is familiar to anyone who works with older members of the community. Fraudsters favour the CRA because it is the one institution almost everyone fears a little, and they favour seniors because many of them live alone, answer the telephone, and were raised to treat authority with deference.

The Canada Revenue Agency's own guidance is unambiguous. It does not demand payment by gift card, prepaid credit card or cryptocurrency. It does not threaten arrest. It does not insist that you stay on the line.

The newer variant arrives by text message or, increasingly, by a QR code on a flyer or parking notice. Scanning it opens a page that looks entirely legitimate and asks, politely, for a card number.

This is part of the reason Pinoy Pages verifies businesses in person and requires a mutual vouch from another verified business before a badge appears. A badge is not a guarantee about anyone's honesty. It is a narrower and more useful assurance: somebody visited this business, confirmed it is real and operating, and another established business was willing to put its own name beside it.

The [ZCard](/card/tax-01) that every Verified business receives has a deliberately limited repertoire. It shows who the person is, their badge and verification date, and buttons to call or save the contact. It never asks for a login, a payment or personal information. If a page that resembles ours asks for any of those things, it is not ours.

Maria Santos of [Bituin Tax & Bookkeeping](/listing/tax-01) spends part of every March fielding calls from clients who have received suspicious messages. "My rule is simple," she says. "If anyone tells you that you must pay right now, hang up and call someone you trust. The real CRA will still be there tomorrow."

The habit worth teaching is equally simple. Before tapping a QR code, read the address that appears in the preview. Ours says pinoypages.ca.
