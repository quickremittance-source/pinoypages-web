# First 30 days in Canada: SIN, health card, bank account, phone

_From Pinoy Pages 2026–2027_

Welcome, kabayan. Your first month is about four practical basics: a **Social Insurance Number (SIN)** so you can work and get paid, **provincial health coverage**, a **bank account**, and a **Canadian phone number** so employers, banks and government can reach you. Rules differ by province. This guide starts with federal steps that apply everywhere, then Ontario (home to many Pinoy Pages readers), with links for Alberta, British Columbia and Manitoba.

## 1. Get your Social Insurance Number (SIN)

A SIN is a 9-digit number you need to **work in Canada**, get paid, pay taxes, and access programs such as Employment Insurance and the Canada Pension Plan. There is **no fee** to apply.

**Who can apply:** Anyone 12 or older can apply for their own SIN. A parent or legal guardian can apply for a child under the age of majority.

**Documents:** You generally need **one primary** identity document that proves your legal status in Canada, and **one secondary** document that confirms your identity. Temporary residents need a valid IRCC work permit, study permit or visitor record that authorizes work. Emails from IRCC are **not** accepted as the permit itself. SINs issued to temporary residents **start with 9** and expire with the permit.

**How to apply (fastest first):**
- **Online** through Service Canada (about **5 business days** when complete)
- **By mail** (about **20 business days**)
- **In person** at a Service Canada Centre (you can often get your SIN during the visit if everything is in order)

Protect your SIN. Service Canada no longer issues plastic SIN cards. View or print your confirmation through My Service Canada Account when available.

**Source:** [Apply for a SIN](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/sin/apply.html), [Required documents](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/sin/required-documents.html), [SIN for temporary residents](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/sin/temporary-residents.html), [Application status](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/sin/status.html)

## 2. Apply for provincial health coverage

Health insurance is **provincial or territorial**, not federal. Apply as soon as you settle. Coverage rules and wait times differ.

### Ontario (OHIP)

Ontario’s plan is the **Ontario Health Insurance Plan (OHIP)**. There is **no waiting period**: if you are eligible, coverage can start when you are approved. Apply **in person at ServiceOntario** with:
- a completed Registration for Ontario Health Insurance Coverage form
- **three separate** qualifying documents: proof of Canadian citizenship or OHIP-eligible immigration status; proof you live in Ontario; proof of identity (photocopies not accepted; check which List 2 items may be digital)

**Minimum presence rules** include making Ontario your primary residence and being physically in Ontario for **153 days** in any 12-month period, and for **153 of the first 183 days** after you begin living here. You must also meet at least one additional category (for example permanent resident, certain work-permit holders working full-time for an Ontario employer for at least six months, convention refugee/protected person, and others listed on ontario.ca).

**Source:** [Apply for OHIP and get a health card](https://www.ontario.ca/page/apply-ohip-and-get-health-card)

### Other provinces (check your own page)

- **Alberta:** [Apply for AHCIP coverage](https://www.alberta.ca/ahcip-apply)
- **British Columbia:** [Apply for MSP](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/health-drug-coverage/msp/bc-residents/eligibility-and-enrolment/apply-for-msp) (newcomers may face a waiting period; confirm on the BC site and keep private insurance if needed)
- **Manitoba:** [Moving to Manitoba — health coverage](https://www.gov.mb.ca/health/mhsip/movingtomanitoba.html)

## 3. Open a bank account

Federally regulated banks must confirm your identity before opening an account. You must provide **original** identification, not copies.

**Option 1:** two documents from reliable sources — one with your **name and address**, one with your **name and date of birth** (for example government ID, recent tax or benefits notices, utility bills, bank/credit statements, or a foreign passport, as listed by FCAC).

**Option 2:** one document showing name and date of birth, plus identity confirmation by a bank customer in good standing or a person of good standing in the community.

You can open an account if you are not a Canadian citizen, with proper identification. Some banks may ask you to visit a branch in person.

**Newcomers:** under the Commitment on Low-Cost and No-Cost Accounts, a **no-cost account** may be available for your **first year in Canada** (same core features as low-cost accounts, with no monthly fee). Low-cost accounts are capped at a **$4** monthly fee or less and must include a minimum set of services. Compare total costs (transactions, ATM, NSF, foreign-currency fees), not only the monthly fee.

**Source:** [Opening a bank account](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/banking/opening-bank-account.html), [Low-cost or no-cost accounts](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/banking/bank-accounts/low-cost-no-cost.html)

## 4. Get a Canadian phone number

Employers, banks, Service Canada, the CRA and IRCC often need a Canadian number for callbacks and security codes. There is no single federal “get a phone” program. Practical steps many newcomers use:

- Compare prepaid and postpaid plans from major carriers and mobile virtual network operators
- Bring passport and secondary ID; some plans need a Canadian address or a deposit if you have no credit history yet
- Keep the same number when possible so your SIN, bank and benefit accounts stay reachable

Once you have a number and a bank account, set up **direct deposit** for pay and for CRA benefits later.

## Suggested order for week one

1. SIN (online if you can)
2. Phone number you can receive texts and calls on
3. Bank account (bring originals)
4. Provincial health application (Ontario: ServiceOntario in person)

Then bookmark My Service Canada Account and, after your first tax Notice of Assessment, CRA My Account.
