# Sending money home: what to compare

_From Pinoy Pages 2026–2027_

Sending money to family in the Philippines (a **remittance**) is part of many kabayan households. Costs are easy to miss if you only look at the advertised “fee.” The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) says to compare the **total cost**: transfer fees, exchange-rate costs, and charges the recipient may pay. This guide follows that checklist. **It does not rank providers** and does not invent rates.

## How a remittance works (FCAC)

A business or financial institution in Canada sends the transaction details to an agent, business or financial institution abroad. The recipient may get **cash**, a **bank deposit**, or a credit to a **card**. You can send through banks, credit unions, money transfer businesses, cheque-cashing businesses and currency exchanges — sometimes through outlets in post offices, grocery stores, pharmacies or convenience stores.

**Source:** [Sending money to someone in another country](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/payment/international-money-transfers.html)

## What to compare before you send

### 1. Transfer fees

Fees vary by provider, amount and payment method. Federally regulated financial institutions in Canada must tell you how much **they** will charge to send money abroad. That disclosed fee **does not** include charges that may apply in the other country.

### 2. Exchange rate (FX margin)

Some businesses profit by using a less favourable exchange rate than the mid-market rate. FCAC research on banking fees discusses **currency conversion markup**. Ask what rate you get, and compare it with other quotes for the **same** amount, currencies and payout method. Ask whether the recipient’s amount is locked when **you pay** or when **they collect**.

For a public reference rate (not the rate a provider will give you), see the [Bank of Canada exchange rates](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/).

### 3. Recipient-side charges

The person receiving the money may pay a fee or tax to collect it. Prepaid-card payouts may add usage fees. Ask before you send.

### 4. Speed vs cost

Delivery can take from a few **minutes** to a few **days**. Faster options usually cost more. Decide whether the extra cost is worth it for this transfer.

### 5. How the recipient collects

Confirm there is a practical pickup or deposit option where your family lives, and what **ID** they need.

### 6. How you pay

Paying by **credit card** may cost more: the transfer may count as a **cash advance**, with interest starting right away and a higher rate than purchases.

## Trust and complaints

FCAC suggests checking experiences with family and community, and with consumer protection offices. Federally regulated institutions must have a complaint process. Keep your **receipt and transaction number**.
