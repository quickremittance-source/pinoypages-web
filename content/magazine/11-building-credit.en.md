# Building credit in Canada

_From Pinoy Pages 2026–2027_

In Canada, landlords, phone companies, lenders and sometimes employers look at your **credit report** and **credit score**. Credit history from the Philippines or another country usually does **not** transfer automatically. Canada’s two main credit bureaus — **Equifax** and **TransUnion** — mainly track credit activity **in Canada**. Some lenders may ask to see a foreign credit report; that is between you and the lender.

This guide sticks to official Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) guidance. It does **not** rank banks or credit cards.

## What a credit report is

Your credit report summarizes how you have used credit: cards, loans, mortgages, how much you owe, and whether you pay on time. Lenders send account information to the bureaus. Reports can also include collections, certain court decisions, and inquiries from the last three years. Bureaus update reports **at least monthly**.

**Checking your own report or score does not hurt your score.**

**Source:** [Credit report and score basics](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/credit-report-score-basics.html)

## How to get your free report

You can get a free credit report from both Equifax and TransUnion **online**, **by mail**, or in some places **in person** (bring at least two valid pieces of ID). FCAC links to each bureau’s free-report process.

Phone lines listed by FCAC:
- Equifax Canada: **1-800-465-7166**
- TransUnion Canada: **1-800-663-9980**

**Source:** [Getting your credit report and credit score](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/order-credit-report.html)

## What improves a score (FCAC)

FCAC highlights that **payment history** is the most important factor:

- Pay on time; at least the minimum if you cannot pay in full
- Contact the lender early if you will have trouble
- Do not skip a payment even while disputing a charge

Other FCAC points:

- Keep balances **below** your credit limit (utilization)
- Prefer a **long, stable** history: keep older no-fee accounts open and occasionally active when you can manage them
- Opening many new accounts, or closing an old account after a balance transfer, can lower the average age of accounts and available credit

Borrow only what you can repay. Review interest, fees and terms before you sign. Paying for a purchase by credit card and carrying a balance can cost more than the sticker price.

**Source:** [Improving your credit score](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/improve-credit-score.html), [What to consider before borrowing](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/consider-borrow.html)

## How long information stays (high level)

FCAC notes that positive paid accounts can stay for many years (Equifax: open accounts while open, closed accounts up to about 10 years; TransUnion: information may stay up to about 20 years even if closed). Exact retention for negative items varies — read the FCAC page and each bureau’s disclosure for your province.

**Source:** [How long information stays on your credit report](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/information-credit-report.html)

## Newcomer-friendly starting points (no rankings)

Without naming preferred products, common building blocks that create a Canadian file when used responsibly include:

- A credit product you can repay in full each month
- On-time payments for any loan or line of credit you take
- Avoiding many hard inquiries in a short period when you do not need them

If a bank refuses a product, ask what would help later, keep building a clean payment record, and check both bureau reports for errors.
