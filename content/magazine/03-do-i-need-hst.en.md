# Do I need to register for HST? The $30,000 small supplier rule, explained

_From Pinoy Pages 2026–2027_

You started cleaning houses on weekends. Or you went independent as a caregiver, a nurse, a bookkeeper, a graphic designer. Or you're selling lumpia trays from home. At some point someone asks: **"Are you charging HST?"** Here's how the rule works, in plain language.

## The short answer

Most businesses **must register for GST/HST once they are no longer a "small supplier."** You stop being a small supplier when your **taxable sales go over $30,000**, counted either:

1. **in one calendar quarter**, or
2. **across the last four calendar quarters in a row** (or fewer).

If you stay at **$30,000 or less** over four consecutive calendar quarters, you're a small supplier. You **don't have to register**, but you can choose to.

## What counts toward the $30,000

- Your **total revenue before expenses** from taxable sales. That's **gross**, not profit.
- If you're a **sole proprietor**, add up **all your businesses** (and those of any "associates"), including sales made **inside and outside Canada**.
- **Zero-rated** sales (taxed at 0%) **do count**.
- **Don't include** financial services, sales of capital property (like business equipment), or goodwill from selling a business.

"Calendar quarters" are January–March, April–June, July–September and October–December.

## When you stop being a small supplier

| Situation                                                                                   | When you're no longer a small supplier                      | What to do                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| You go **over $30,000 in a single quarter**                                                 | On the **sale that took you over** $30,000                  | Charge GST/HST on that sale and register. Your registration takes effect no later than that sale. |
| You go **over $30,000 across four (or fewer) consecutive quarters**, but not in one quarter | At the **end of the month after** the quarter you went over | Register. Start charging GST/HST from your first sale after that point.                           |

In both cases, the CRA says you have to **register within 29 days** of your effective date of registration.

### A simple illustration (made-up numbers, based on the CRA's own examples)

Maria starts a bookkeeping side business in January:

| Quarter   | Sales       |
| --------- | ----------- |
| Jan–Mar   | $6,000      |
| Apr–Jun   | $9,000      |
| Jul–Sep   | $10,000     |
| Oct–Dec   | $8,000      |
| **Total** | **$33,000** |

She goes over $30,000 across four quarters during October–December. She stops being a small supplier at the **end of January** (the end of the month after that quarter). From her first sale after that, she charges HST and needs to register within 29 days.

## Special cases

- **Rideshare and taxi drivers** must register for GST/HST **from their first fare, even if they're small suppliers.**
- If you only make **exempt** supplies, you generally **can't** register.
- If your **temporary SIN starts with 9**, the CRA says to register through **Business Registration Online (BRO)**.

## What rate do I charge in Ontario?

For most taxable sales where the place of supply is Ontario, the HST rate is **13%**. Some items, like basic groceries, are **zero-rated (0%)**, and some services are **exempt**. Check before you invoice.

## Should I register voluntarily?

Some small suppliers choose to register so they can claim **input tax credits** (the GST/HST paid on business expenses). It also means charging your clients HST, filing returns, and keeping good records. Talk it through with a qualified tax professional before you decide.

## Once you're registered

- Hold the HST you collect **in trust**. It's not your money to spend.
- File and pay on your schedule (see our [2027 tax deadlines](/guides/2027-tax-deadlines)).
- Keep your records, usually for **six years**.
