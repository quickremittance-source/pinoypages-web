# The account built for a first home, and how families are using it

Ben Ocampo's parents rented the same basement off Steeles Avenue for eleven years. They saved diligently and never quite assembled a down payment, partly because nobody ever explained to them which accounts would help.

"That's the reason I do this work," says Ocampo, a mortgage agent with [Tahanan Mortgage Partners](/listing/ins-05) in Markham. "Most of my meetings now take place at a kitchen table, often with a grandparent listening from the sofa, because in our families the down payment is frequently a joint project."

The instrument he discusses most is the First Home Savings Account, or FHSA, introduced in 2023. It combines two attractive features. Contributions are tax-deductible, much like an RRSP, and a qualifying withdrawal to buy a first home is not taxed, much like a TFSA.

The limits are modest by design. An individual may contribute up to $8,000 a year [verify 2027 figure], to a lifetime maximum of $40,000 [verify 2027 figure], and a limited amount of unused room carries forward to the following year. The account can stay open for a set number of years [verify 2027 figure], after which unused savings can generally be moved to an RRSP without losing the deduction.

Couples should note that the limits apply per person. Two first-time buyers can each open an account, which matters considerably at GTA prices.

The FHSA can also be combined with the RRSP Home Buyers' Plan, which permits a withdrawal from an RRSP towards a first home that must be repaid over time [verify 2027 figure]. Used together, the two can make a meaningful difference, although the rules on eligibility and timing are precise and worth reviewing with a professional before acting.

Joy Fernandez of [Sinag Accounting](/listing/tax-05) in Scarborough sees the tax side. "People open the account and then forget to claim the deduction, or they claim it in the wrong year," she says. "You are allowed to carry the deduction forward to a year when your income is higher. That is often the sensible choice for someone early in their career."

Neither of them pretends the arithmetic is easy. Rents remain high, and saving while paying them is genuinely difficult. Ocampo's advice is modest: open the account early, even with a small contribution, so the contribution room begins to accumulate.
