# Caregiver to permanent residence: what IRCC shows right now

_From Pinoy Pages 2026–2027_

Many kababayan come to Canada to care for children, seniors or people with disabilities, hoping to become permanent residents. **Programs change.** This page only summarizes what Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) published as of the check date. It is **not** an application guide and **not** legal advice. Always read the live IRCC pages before you spend money on forms or a job offer.

## Permanent residence pathways for caregivers — current status

On IRCC’s main caregivers page, these programs are marked **Closed** (or closed to new applicants):

| Program | IRCC status (as published) |
| --- | --- |
| **Home Care Worker Immigration pilots** (Child Care and Home Support) | **Closed** — apply pages show Closed; intake paused |
| **Home Child Care Provider Pilot** and **Home Support Worker Pilot** | Closed — last day to apply was **June 17, 2024** |
| **Live-in Caregiver Program** | Closed to new applicants except limited situations described by IRCC (work permit already based on an LMIA submitted before December 2014) |
| **Caring for Children Pilot** | Closed — last day **June 17, 2019** |
| **Caring for People with High Medical Needs Pilot** | Closed — last day **June 17, 2019** |
| **Interim Pathway for Caregivers** | Closed — last day **October 8, 2019** |

### Home Care Worker Immigration pilots — intake pause

IRCC announced on **December 19, 2025** that, because demand exceeded available spaces and wait times were growing, it would **pause intake** of the Home Care Worker Immigration pilots **until further notice**, and that intake would **not re-open in March 2026**. IRCC said it would continue processing applications already received, in line with the Immigration Levels Plan, and would share any future updates publicly.

**Source:** [Caregivers (IRCC)](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/caregivers.html), [Home Care Worker Immigration pilots](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/caregivers/home-care-worker-immigration-pilots.html), [Pausing Home Care Worker Immigration pilots application intake](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/news/notices/pausing-home-care-worker-immigration-pilots-application-intake.html)

**If you already applied** under a pilot before intake closed or paused: use IRCC’s status tools and account linking pages; processing continues for applications received to date per IRCC’s notice. Do not assume a new intake window until IRCC publishes one.

## Temporary work as a home care worker (still described by IRCC)

Separately from permanent residence, IRCC describes how some people may work temporarily as a **home child care provider (NOC 44100)** or **home support worker (NOC 44101)** when an employer has a positive **Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA)** under the Temporary Foreign Worker Program. IRCC notes this type of caregiver work-permit application currently gets **faster processing**, with eligibility limits (for example, applying from outside Canada through TFWP is described for work **in Quebec** in the cases IRCC lists; visitors in Canada generally cannot use this route except where IRCC says Quebec work may still qualify).

Your employer needs a **positive LMIA** before you apply for the work permit. Extending or changing jobs also generally needs a new positive LMIA first. If you applied to extend before expiry, ask IRCC about **maintained status**.

**Source:** [Work temporarily as a caregiver](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/caregivers/work-temporarily-caregiver.html), [Hire a home care worker](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/hire-home-care-worker.html)

## What this means in plain language

- **Do not rely on closed pilots** for a new permanent residence plan.
- **Watch canada.ca** for any future caregiver PR intake — Pinoy Pages will not invent reopening dates.
- Temporary LMIA-based work and permanent residence are **different** processes; a job offer or work permit alone is not permanent residence.
- For personal advice, use a [regulated Canadian immigration consultant or lawyer](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigration-citizenship-representative.html) authorized to practise.
