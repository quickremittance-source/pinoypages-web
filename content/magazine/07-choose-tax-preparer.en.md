# How to choose a tax preparer: red flags and questions to ask

_From Pinoy Pages 2026–2027_

In our community, we often find a tax preparer through a kumare, a church friend or a Facebook group. Referrals are great, but **you're still responsible for everything on your return, even if someone else prepares it.** Here's how to choose well. _(We don't recommend specific firms. Being listed in our directory is not an endorsement.)_

## 🚩 Red flags

Walk away, or at least get a second opinion, if a preparer:

- **Promises an unusually large refund** before seeing your documents
- **Offers false claims**, like donations you didn't make, childcare you didn't pay for, or business expenses or losses that aren't real
- **Takes their fee as a share of your refund** as part of a "tax savings" deal
- Asks you to **sign a blank form**, or won't show you the return before filing
- **Won't give you a copy** of your return
- **Discourages you from getting a second opinion or talking to the CRA**
- Says their scheme is **"approved by the CRA"**, or pitches a tax-saving **investment or "business opportunity"** at a seminar or online
- Asks for your **CRA sign-in password**. Proper access goes through the CRA's formal **authorization** process.

The CRA has a specific warning for **newcomers**: tax schemes target people who are new to the system. If it sounds too good to be true, it probably is.

## ❓ Questions to ask before you hire

1. **What are your qualifications and experience?** Do you hold a professional designation or licence? (You can verify it with the body that issued it.)
2. **Have you done returns like mine?** For example: self-employed, a newcomer with foreign income, rental income, or a small corporation.
3. **What will it cost, and what's included?** Get the fee in writing before they start.
4. **Will you file electronically (EFILE)?** The CRA keeps a **tax preparer directory** of preparers certified to EFILE.
5. **Will I review the return and Form T183 before I sign?** (You should, every time.)
6. **What access to my CRA account will you ask for, and for how long?**
7. **If the CRA reviews my return or sends a letter, will you help? Is that included?**
8. **How do you keep my documents and SIN secure?**
9. **Are you a "discounter"?** Discounters pay you part of your expected refund up front. Their fees are **regulated**. Make sure they have your address so any extra refund can be sent to you.

## ✅ Your side of the deal

- **Check your CRA info first**: address, direct deposit, notification preferences
- **Bring everything**: all slips, receipts, and records of self-employment income and expenses
- **Review the return** and the information on **Form T183**. They must match. Then sign **Part F** to authorize filing.
- **Keep copies** of your slips, receipts and T183 for **at least six years**
- **Check your Notice of Assessment.** It usually arrives within about **two weeks** for e-filed returns.

## Free options

If you have a **modest income and a simple tax situation**, a **free tax clinic** run by community volunteers (the CVITP) may be able to file for you. If you're **self-employed or own a small business**, the CRA's **Liaison Officer Service** offers free help understanding your tax obligations.
