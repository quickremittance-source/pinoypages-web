# Simbang Gabi in Canada: an explainer

_From Pinoy Pages 2026–2027_

**Simbang Gabi** (also called **Misa de Gallo**, “Rooster’s Mass”) is a Filipino Catholic Christmas tradition: a **novena of nine Masses** in the days leading up to Christmas. In the Philippines it is often celebrated in the **pre-dawn** hours. This piece explains the tradition from documented cultural and parish sources. It does **not** invent Mass times for Canadian churches — those are set by each parish each year.

## What it is

Field documentation summarized by **Catholics & Cultures** (College of the Holy Cross) describes Simbang Gabi as nine mornings of Mass before Christmas. Churches fill early; some celebrate outdoors when crowds overflow. Rising in the dark and travelling to church is part of the sense of sacrifice and anticipation. Many devotees treat completed attendance as a **panata** (vow) and bring petitions to the child Jesus across the nine days.

The practice grew from Spanish **Misas de Aguinaldo / Misas de Gallo** held before dawn for field labourers in honour of the Annunciation. The pre-dawn novena largely faded in Spain and much of the world but remained a living custom in the Philippines.

**Source:** [Simbang Gabi, pre-dawn Masses… | Catholics & Cultures](https://www.catholicsandcultures.org/philippines/christmas-epiphany/simbang-gabi) (published May 15, 2015; last modified May 19, 2025)

## Food and fellowship

After Mass in the Philippines, communities often share seasonal foods — classically **bibingka**, **puto bumbong** and other rice cakes, coffee, *pan de sal* or *arroz caldo* — whether from an *agape* meal or from vendors outside the church. Catholics & Cultures notes that some traditional foods have been partly replaced by other snacks, but the social gathering remains central.

## How it usually works in Canada

There is **no single national Simbang Gabi calendar** issued by the Government of Canada. Filipino Catholics in Canada typically:

- Watch announcements from **local Catholic parishes** and dioceses with Filipino apostolate or multicultural ministries
- Attend Masses scheduled in the **evening** after work as well as early morning, depending on the parish
- Share *bibingka* and *puto bumbong* at parish halls when volunteers organize them

Pinoy Pages’ [events calendar](/calendar) (when live) is meant to list verified parish and community Simbang Gabi schedules that organizers submit — always confirm with the parish before you go.

## If you want to take part

1. Search your city’s Catholic diocese site or parish bulletin for “Simbang Gabi” or “Misa de Gallo” from mid-November onward
2. Ask Filipino chaplaincies, Knights of Columbus councils, or parish Filipino associations
3. Bring a warm coat for outdoor processions or early starts; schedules vary by year and host parish

Simbang Gabi is a **religious and cultural** practice. Non-Catholics are generally welcome to attend Mass respectfully; Communion practice follows Catholic norms set by the parish.
